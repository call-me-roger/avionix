# Systems controls — competitor and customer research (F-24)

## 1. Hardware reference panels

**Logitech/Saitek Pro Flight Switch Panel** — the de-facto hardware reference for this control set. It has 15 switches covering lighting, pitot heat, and fuel pump, a 5-position magneto dial (R / L / BOTH-ALL / START, with OFF), and a physical gear lever with LEDs that show real-time gear status read from the sim (FSX/P3D originally; works with X-Plane via plugins). ([Logitech G product page](https://www.logitechg.com/en-hk/products/flight/flight-simulator-switch-panel.html); [PilotMall](https://www.pilotmall.com/products/saitek-pro-flight-switch-panel); [Amazon UK listing](https://www.amazon.co.uk/Logitech-Professional-Simulation-Controller-Five-Position/dp/B01MQ0GFUH)). Build quality is generally praised, but a recurring complaint across retailer review aggregations is **lack of backlighting on the switches** (bad for night ops) and inconsistent plug-and-play reliability/driver setup on some systems (*unverified which specific units/OS — aggregated from retailer review summaries, not individually dated reviews*). ([MyPilotStore reviews index](https://www.mypilotstore.com/mypilotstore/reviews/product.asp?sku=6359))

**Honeycomb Bravo Throttle Quadrant** — all-in-one quadrant covering GA and airliner configurations: 6 GA + 6 commercial throttle levers (airliner set includes flaps/spoilers/reversers), a gear lever with green/red gear-status LEDs, a 2-way GA flap switch as an alternative to the lever, a GA-style elevator trim wheel, and a 14-light annunciator panel for system status/warnings. ([Honeycomb product page](https://flyhoneycomb.com/products/bravo-throttle-quadrant); [x-plained hardware review](https://www.x-plained.com/hardware-review-honeycomb-bravo-throttle-quadrant/)). Reviewer praise: lever movement is "smooth and precise," friction is adjustable to prevent throttle creep, and the trim wheel "feels good" with "no slack at all." Complaints: the flap lever handle has **no position markings**, and the sim's flap detents don't line up with any marking on the physical handle, so pilots can't tell flap position by feel/look; the annunciator lights can't be manually assigned and show whatever the loaded aircraft happens to drive, which can look arbitrary; commercial throttle handles feel "a bit small"; trim wheel motion felt "a bit light" despite being responsive. ([x-plained](https://www.x-plained.com/hardware-review-honeycomb-bravo-throttle-quadrant/), [nerdtechy first-look](https://nerdtechy.com/honeycomb-bravo-throttle-quadrant-review))

**Honeycomb Alpha** (yoke + switch panel) — has magneto selector, red MASTER BAT/ALT switches, white BUS1/BUS2 AVIONICS switches, and 2-way toggles for BCN/LAND/TAXI/NAV/STROBE lights. Reviewers call the switches "durable" with good tactile feel despite plastic construction. ([x-plained Alpha review](https://www.x-plained.com/hardware-review-honeycomb-alpha-flight-controls/)) Complaints, consistent across multiple independent sources: the **magneto switch is placed oddly close to the yoke shaft/horn**, so the yoke handle "is easily caught" when reaching for it; thumb rocker switches on the handle are oversized and block access to the hat switch, forcing users to lift their thumb over them repeatedly ("not comfortable and user-friendly"); users have also reported the in-sim magneto switch **rapidly cycling on/off** when going from OFF→BOTH, and the starter firing unintentionally alongside the magneto switch due to mapping/axis-overlap issues. ([flightsimtimes "What I dislike in Honeycomb Alpha Yoke Part 2"](http://flightsimtimes.com/2020/05/03/576-what-i-dislike-in-honeycomb-alpha-yoke-part-2/); [AVSIM forum thread title referencing the Alpha magneto switch](https://www.avsim.com/forums/topic/569091-honeycomb-alpha-yoke-magneto-switch/) — thread content unverified, page returned 403 on fetch; [x-plained Alpha review](https://www.x-plained.com/hardware-review-honeycomb-alpha-flight-controls/) noting default X-Plane config gaps, e.g. AVIONICS BUS2 switch left unassigned out of the box).

## 2. Software / touch competitors

**Touch Portal X-Plane plugins** (XPlaneTouchPortalPlugin by coussini; XP-FlightDeck) subscribe to X-Plane datarefs continuously and push live values to the Touch Portal tiles — i.e. tiles are driven by **actual sim state**, not by "last pressed," and update automatically when anything changes the dataref (sim internal logic, a checklist, another control). ([coussini/XPlaneTouchPortalPlugin GitHub](https://github.com/coussini/XPlaneTouchPortalPlugin); [forum thread](https://forums.x-plane.org/forums/topic/336773-control-your-cockpit-and-sim-system-on-your-tablet-x-plane-12-touch-portal-plugin/)). XP-FlightDeck explicitly markets "two-way sync: tiles reflect cockpit states in real time" and ships "ready-made actions and pages for common aircraft controls" grouped by operational category (lighting, brakes, avionics power, etc.), but currently only documents full support for the default Cessna 172SP. ([XP-FlightDeck site](https://www.xp-flightdeck.com/en/))

**Stream Deck plugins** (xp_streamdeck by rwellinger; PilotsDeck by Fragtality) show the most mature pattern for this exact control set:
- **Gear**: a "DataRef Toggle" button bound to the gear handle dataref, rendered as two images (gear up / gear down) driven by live state, not by last tap. ([xp_streamdeck GitHub](https://github.com/rwellinger/xp_streamdeck))
- **Flaps**: a "DataRef Write" action sets a specific detent value (e.g. 0.5) directly, with the button optionally showing current flap position as a live percentage. ([xp_streamdeck GitHub](https://github.com/rwellinger/xp_streamdeck))
- **Trim**: a "Rotary DataRef" action increments/decrements the trim dataref by a configurable step, with separate fine (short press) vs. coarse (long/hold) deltas and optional min/max clamps — i.e. hold-to-trim with rate control, not a single discrete toggle. ([xp_streamdeck GitHub](https://github.com/rwellinger/xp_streamdeck))
- **Magneto/starter**: a "Rotary" enum action cycles OFF→R→L→BOTH→START while displaying current position; critically, it supports a "HOLD on last position" mode that fires a separate begin/end command for the starter only while physically held at START — reproducing the spring-loaded hardware behavior in software. ([xp_streamdeck GitHub](https://github.com/rwellinger/xp_streamdeck))
- PilotsDeck adds a "Korry Button" action specifically for overhead-panel annunciator/switch combos (independent sub-images for lit vs. unlit state) and a generic "Hold Switch"/"Reset Switch" primitive (press-and-hold sends a distinct on-command, release sends off; "Reset Switch" pulses on then off after 100ms) — built for momentary/guarded switches. ([PilotsDeck GitHub](https://github.com/Fragtality/PilotsDeck))

**Air Manager (Sim Innovations)** connects to X-Plane/FSX/P3D over the network, subscribes to datarefs/variables, and offers a touch/mouse input mode for its skinned instrument panels. A reviewer using a Surface Pro praised single-handed touch operation (e.g. sliding to tune a radio) as especially useful for helicopter pilots who need one hand on the cyclic. Complaints: too many separate panel windows open at once ("the amount of windows opened are also quite a lot"), no way to set a background texture spanning a whole panel, and no way to export/share panel layouts between computers. ([HeliSimmer review](https://www.helisimmer.com/reviews/panels-made-easy-air-manager); [Air Manager 4.x manual](https://siminnovations.com/wiki/index.php/Air_Manager_4.x_User_Manual))

**FS-FlightControl** is positioned as an instructor-station / second-screen app (moving map, weather, pushback, fuel/load, failures) rather than a dedicated systems-switch panel; it is touch-optimized for tablets and rated ~4.1/5 on app stores, but systems-switch coverage specifically is not its core focus. ([Google Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.fs_flightcontrol.client&hl=en_AU&gl=US); [HeliSimmer review](https://www.helisimmer.com/reviews/fs-flightcontrol-fsx-p3d-x-plane))

**XpRemotePanel** is a free iPad/Android touch instrument panel for X-Plane 11/12 focused on instruments and autopilot/FMS/CDU/NAV displays rather than a systems-switch panel. ([senecaflyer.com](https://senecaflyer.com/xpremotepanel/); [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/xpremotepanel/id1576583318))

**Mobiflight** (primarily physical-hardware/Arduino bridge, not touch, but relevant for state-sync lessons): users report that **bi-state switches are a known pain point** — a 3-position (ON-OFF-ON) physical switch can't be safely synced back to a simulator's internal state by Mobiflight's "synchro" tool unless it starts from a known position, and that MSFS and X-Plane model switches differently (X-Plane often has explicit "on"/"off" commands, MSFS often only a "toggle" command), so **a toggle-only command plus a stateful physical/virtual switch can get stuck inverted** relative to the sim if the two ever fall out of step — a core argument for always driving the visual representation from the sim's true state, never from local/last-pressed state. ([MobiFlight forum: 3-way ON-OFF-ON switches](https://www.mobiflight.com/forum/topic/6341.html); [MobiFlight forum: Lights On/Off vs Toggle](https://www.mobiflight.com/forum/topic/6464.html))

**SimHub, XP-FlightDeck's XP-FlightDeck/XPanel, Flight Deck ONE** — referenced in the research brief but no substantive, verifiable review/feature detail on systems-control handling was found within the search budget available for this report; treat as **unverified / needs follow-up research**.

## 3. What customers like and dislike (cross-cutting themes)

- **State sync is the #1 recurring friction point.** Hardware panels can show stale/incorrect state if not driven by the sim (e.g. Mobiflight's documented ON-OFF-ON sync problem); software plugins that succeeded (xp_streamdeck, PilotsDeck, Touch Portal plugins) all explicitly subscribe to live datarefs and render from them, not from the last button press. ([MobiFlight forum](https://www.mobiflight.com/forum/topic/6341.html); [xp_streamdeck](https://github.com/rwellinger/xp_streamdeck))
- **Touch + simulator interop is fragile at the OS/engine level.** MSFS forum users report touch input on popped-out panels (e.g. G3X Touch) registering as an untargeted mouse click, or requiring a mouse-move to "release" after a touch — a platform-level touch/mouse conflict, not an app design choice, but a reminder that touch targets need generous hit-areas and forgiving hit-testing. ([MSFS forums: Touch Screen issues with Pop out Panels](https://forums.flightsimulator.com/t/touch-screen-issues-with-pop-out-panels/217825))
- **Spring-loaded/momentary controls need explicit software modeling.** The starter position of a magneto rotary is not a simple toggle; xp_streamdeck's "HOLD on last position" with separate begin/end starter commands exists specifically because naive toggle mapping misbehaves (sim starter staying engaged, or needing a second a press to release). ([xp_streamdeck GitHub](https://github.com/rwellinger/xp_streamdeck))
- **Discrete positions need direct-write, not increment-cycling, for predictability.** Flap handling via "DataRef Write to exact detent value" (xp_streamdeck) avoids the ambiguity of a lever with no physical position markings (a direct complaint about the Bravo's flap handle). ([x-plained Bravo review](https://www.x-plained.com/hardware-review-honeycomb-bravo-throttle-quadrant/); [xp_streamdeck GitHub](https://github.com/rwellinger/xp_streamdeck))
- **Ergonomic/placement complaints recur around tight clusters of switches near other moving controls** (Alpha's magneto switch near the yoke shaft catching the yoke handle) — relevant analog for touch: don't place frequently-hit toggles immediately adjacent to a draggable/continuous control (e.g. trim slider) where a drag could clip a toggle. ([flightsimtimes](http://flightsimtimes.com/2020/05/03/576-what-i-dislike-in-honeycomb-alpha-yoke-part-2/))
- **Backlighting/night-visibility is a named gap on hardware** (Saitek panel lacks switch backlighting), suggesting dark-cockpit/night-mode legibility is valued and often under-delivered. ([MyPilotStore reviews](https://www.mypilotstore.com/mypilotstore/reviews/product.asp?sku=6359))
- **Clutter/window-sprawl is a named complaint for multi-panel software** (Air Manager's "amount of windows opened are also quite a lot"), arguing for a single organized panel/tab surface rather than many independent floating sub-panels. ([HeliSimmer Air Manager review](https://www.helisimmer.com/reviews/panels-made-easy-air-manager))
- Annunciator/warning lights that fire for reasons the user doesn't expect or can't configure (Bravo's non-assignable annunciators lighting up "unexpectedly") reduce trust in the panel — state should be explicable, not just "whatever the aircraft happens to drive." ([x-plained Bravo review](https://www.x-plained.com/hardware-review-honeycomb-bravo-throttle-quadrant/))

## 4. Implications for Avionix

1. **Always render switch/lever/dial position from live X-Plane dataref state, never from the last tap recorded locally.** This is the single most validated lesson across both the hardware-sync failures (Mobiflight ON-OFF-ON problem) and the software successes (xp_streamdeck, PilotsDeck, Touch Portal plugins) that explicitly subscribe to datarefs and re-render on change.
2. **Model the magneto/starter as a rotary with a momentary START detent**, not a simple 5-state toggle: press-and-hold engages the starter command, release disengages it, matching xp_streamdeck's "HOLD on last position" pattern and avoiding the "starter fires unexpectedly" complaints seen with hardware mapping.
3. **Write flaps directly to exact detent values** (not up/down increment cycling) and display the resulting detent as a labeled readout (e.g. "FLAPS 2 / 15°"), addressing the Bravo's most-cited flaw: a lever with no legible position indicator.
4. **Use gear-position lights with a clear 3-state model** (in transit / down-and-locked-green / unsafe-red, mirroring the Saitek/Bravo LED convention simmers already expect) rather than a single on/off icon, and drive it from the actual gear deployment dataref, not the handle command.
5. **Implement trim as hold-to-trim with a visible rate indicator and an immediate stop on release** — borrow the fine/coarse step-size pattern from xp_streamdeck's Rotary DataRef action, and make sure releasing the touch control stops the command instantly (no residual "runaway" from a queued repeat).
6. **Group controls by real panel/operational flow (pre-start, taxi, takeoff/landing), echoing both the Bravo's physical left/right split and XP-FlightDeck's "ready-made pages for common controls"**, rather than one undifferentiated grid — this also mitigates the clutter complaint raised against Air Manager's many simultaneous windows.
7. **Keep toggle/lever hit-targets away from drag-style continuous controls** (e.g. don't place a magneto-style toggle flush against a trim slider's drag path) to avoid the "handle catches the switch" class of complaint seen on the Alpha yoke.
8. **Design an explicit dark/night theme for the panel** with strong contrast on switch state, since lack of backlighting is a specifically and repeatedly named shortcoming of the Saitek hardware panel.
9. **Make annunciator/warning indications traceable to a cause** (tooltip or secondary readout showing which dataref/condition is driving the light) rather than an opaque lit/unlit lamp, addressing the Bravo's "lights fire and you don't know why" complaint.
10. **Treat momentary/guarded switches (fuel pump prime, de-ice test, starter) as a distinct interaction class** (press-and-hold / spring-return) separate from simple persistent toggles (lights, pitot heat, avionics master) — PilotsDeck's distinct "Hold Switch" vs. "Dynamic Button" primitives are a useful model to copy conceptually.
